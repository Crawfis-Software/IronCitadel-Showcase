# The Hollowmere Manor Infiltration — level brief

**Premise.** An abandoned manor house in disarray. The player starts in a safe room and has to reach the
master of the house, the level's boss, in the ballroom. They are ill-equipped for it: the guns and blades are
in the gun room, and its entrance is heavily guarded. Do they take it head on (and die), or find the long way
round for a surprise attack?

**Where you are.** At the front door, with the obvious path leading to the guarded gun room. Another path
exists, if you can find it.

**The level, in three acts.**

*Act one is the problem: the choke point.* The player comes in at the foyer, and the obvious way on is
through the gun room. The gun room's entrance on this side is guarded, but the guards come into view only at
its doorway. The foyer is where the player sees that entrance.

*Act two is the way round.* Another way leads round the gun room, through a wing of rooms that belong together
thematically, to a large dining hall. It does not pass through the gun room. A hidden doorway, door or passage
leads to a small strong room. Nothing points to it, and it lies on no way through.

*Act three is the gallery, the flank and the ballroom.* The dining hall is the level's centrepiece: large and
tall, with long tables. It once held great dinners. A minstrels' gallery runs along one side of it, high above
the floor, and ranged enemies hold it: a player who walks out onto the hall floor takes heavy damage. Instead,
the player can climb to the gallery from where they come in, without crossing the hall floor or being seen,
and take out the ranged enemies there first. From its rail, the player sees much of the floor below and has an
advantage in taking out enemies. Beneath the gallery, a way leads into the gun room from behind: a player who
takes it comes in behind the guards watching the entrance, and takes the gun room's best gear. Then they cross
the hall and make their way through more rooms to the ballroom, where the master's chair stands on an elevated
stage at the far end.

**The rooms.** One room of each kind below. You may add a few more where your layout needs them (a passage,
an antechamber); say why. The house was abandoned long ago, and its rooms show it: each is either left to
decay or ransacked by looters. The gun room, still stocked, and the hidden strong room, which nobody found,
are the exceptions. Each room should read as its kind from its shape, its height, its light and what fills it,
not from a label.

- *Foyer.* The safe room where the player starts: the front hall, long deserted, with benches, a coat stand
  and a tall clock. Standard height.
- *Gun room.* Dense rows of gun cabinets and racks of guns, blades and hunting gear, still fully stocked, with
  trophies on the walls. Standard height.
- *Watch room.* The night watchman's post, deserted or wrecked in a brawl: a desk, keys and lamps. Low and
  dim.
- *Cellar cells.* Low and dark. Cells with barred doors in rows, forced in a riot long over; cots and
  furniture overturned.
- *Kitchen.* A cold range, worktables, pans and crockery, raided or left to spoil. Low.
- *Library.* Tall bookcases packed close, with reading tables; books pulled down and scattered, or left to
  rot.
- *Laboratory.* The master's laboratory: benches of glassware and specimen jars, much of it smashed, spilled
  and stained.
- *Boiler room.* Gone cold: a great furnace whose flue climbs to the ceiling, with coal, pipes and tools left
  where they fell.
- *Strong room.* Small, low and sealed. Safes, strongboxes and the family silver, untouched.
- *Dining hall.* As above. Tall and bright, its long tables overturned or still laid under dust, with
  chandeliers hanging from the ceiling and portraits on the walls.
- *Ballroom.* Tall, bright, sparse and grand, though fallen: no room is taller, and none is sparser. A torn
  runner leads across the floor to the stage and the master's chair.
- *Crypt.* The family crypt. Low and dark. Coffins and burial niches, broken open or collapsed.
- *Storeroom.* Trunks, crates and sheeted furniture, plundered or left to rot. Standard height.
- *Chapel.* Tall and still: an altar, candles and crosses, desecrated or in ruins.
- *Butcher's room.* Low and dark. Meat hooks, chains and cages, and a stained block long gone dry.

**What must be true.**

- The gun room has two ways in: its guarded entrance, which the player sees from the foyer, and a way in from
  the dining hall, beneath the gallery.
- A way leads from the foyer to the dining hall without passing through the gun room.
- A gallery runs along one side of the dining hall, high above the floor. The player can reach it from where
  the way round comes into the hall, without crossing the hall floor, and much of the floor can be seen from
  its rail.
- The master's chair stands on a stage with steps.
- The strong room has one way in, and the player may have to search for it: a hidden door, a way behind
  obstacles, or the like.
- The cellar cells, the watch room, the kitchen and the strong room are low. The dining hall and the ballroom
  are tall, and no room is taller than the ballroom.
- Everything is indoors, and every room has a ceiling.
- Every opening between rooms is a door or a doorway, some open and some shut. The player can open any shut
  door on a way through.
- The level has tall and low occluders the player can hide behind, standing or crouching.

**What I don't care about.** How big the level is, and which room sits where. Which layout each room uses, as
long as it reads as its kind. Exact heights, as long as low, standard and tall read as such. Exact counts of
tables, cabinets and bookcases. The colour of the curtains. The seed. Gameplay, animations and NPC behaviour:
the acts say how the level should play, but you build the spaces, not the play.

**Defenders and pickups.** They are markers; nothing moves. Ranged enemies stand behind the gun room's guarded
entrance, facing the foyer, and melee enemies fill the rest of the gun room. Ranged enemies also hold the
gallery. The dining hall holds the largest group, and the master and his bodyguards hold the ballroom. Every
other room holds whoever belongs in it, if anyone; the foyer holds no one. There are two pickups: the gun
room's best gear and the strong room's hoard.
